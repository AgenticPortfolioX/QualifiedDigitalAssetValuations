## When Do You Need a Qualified Appraisal?

The IRS requires a qualified appraisal in the following scenarios:

### Thresholds Requiring Form 8283 Section B

| Donation Type | Fair Market Value | Documentation Required |
|---------------|-------------------|------------------------|
| Cash contributions | Any amount | Bank record or written acknowledgment |
| Ordinary income property | Under $5,000 | Written acknowledgment from charity |
| **Capital gain property (crypto/NFT)** | **$5,000 - $50,000** | **Form 8283 Section A + qualified appraisal** |
| **Capital gain property (crypto/NFT)** | **Over $50,000** | **Form 8283 Section B + qualified appraisal signed by charity** |
| Non-cash contributions (total) | Over $500 | Form 8283 Section A |
| Non-cash contributions (total) | Over $5,000 | **Qualified appraisal required** |

**Key Point:** The $5,000 threshold applies to **each separate item or group of similar items**. If you're donating 10 different NFTs from the same collection, each NFT may be treated as a separate item, potentially requiring individual appraisals if any single NFT exceeds $5,000 in value.

### Digital Assets That Require Qualified Appraisals

- **Bitcoin (BTC)** holdings donated to charity
- **Ethereum (ETH)** and ERC-20 token donations
- **NFTs** from collections like Bored Ape Yacht Club, CryptoPunks, Art Blocks
- **Illiquid tokens** with no active secondary market
- **Staked cryptocurrency** with accrued rewards
- **Crypto mining equipment** (debatable; may be ordinary income property)
- **Tokens held in multiple wallets** (aggregated for threshold determination)

**Exception:** Publicly traded securities (stocks, ETFs) do NOT require a qualified appraisal, even when valued over $5,000, because their fair market value is readily determinable from exchange prices. Cryptocurrency and NFTs do NOT qualify for this exception.

## Understanding IRS Form 8283 for Digital Assets

### Form 8283 Overview

IRS Form 8283, "Noncash Charitable Contributions," must be filed when your total noncash charitable deductions exceed $500. The form has two sections:

- **Section A:** For donations under $5,000 per item
- **Section B:** For donations over $5,000 per item requiring a qualified appraisal

### Section B Requirements (Digital Assets)

When donating cryptocurrency or NFTs valued over $5,000, you must complete Section B and attach:

1. **Completed Form 8283** with your tax return
2. **Qualified appraisal** (received before the due date of your return)
3. **Appraisal summary** (Part V of Form 8283, signed by the appraiser)
4. **Donee acknowledgment** (Part IV of Form 8283, signed by the charity)

### Timeline Requirements

| Event | Deadline |
|-------|----------|
| Receive qualified appraisal | Before filing tax return |
| File Form 8283 with tax return | Tax return due date (including extensions) |
| Appraisal must be dated | No earlier than 60 days before donation |
| Appraisal must be received | No later than tax return due date |

**Critical:** If you file for an extension, you still must receive the qualified appraisal by the original due date (typically April 15), not the extended date.

### Common Form 8283 Errors for Crypto Donations

**Error 1: Using a crypto price tracker screenshot as "valuation"**
- ❌ IRS rejection reason: Does not meet qualified appraisal standards
- ✅ Solution: Obtain USPAP-compliant appraisal from qualified appraiser

**Error 2: Failing to aggregate similar items**
- ❌ If you donate 5 Bitcoin wallets totaling $60,000, treating them as separate $12,000 items without appraisal
- ✅ Solution: Group similar items; if total exceeds $5,000, appraisal required

**Error 3: Incorrect fair market value date**
- ❌ Using the donation date's price vs. using a different date
- ✅ Solution: Fair market value is determined at the **exact date and time of donation** (transaction timestamp)

**Error 4: Donor is also the appraiser**
- ❌ IRS prohibits self-appraisals
- ✅ Solution: Hire independent qualified appraiser with no relationship to donor

## What Makes an Appraiser "Qualified" for Cryptocurrency?

Treasury Regulation Section 1.170A-13(c)(3) defines a qualified appraiser as an individual who meets **ALL** of the following criteria:

### 1. Appraisal Qualifications

- **Holds appropriate certification or designation** from a recognized appraisal organization
- **Regularly performs appraisals** for which they receive compensation
- **Has sufficient knowledge** of digital assets, blockchain technology, and cryptocurrency valuation
- **Demonstrates specialization** in the type of property being appraised (e.g., NFTs vs. Bitcoin)

### 2. Independence Requirements

The appraiser must **NOT** be:

- The donor (you)
- A related party to the donor (family member, business partner, entity you control)
- The donee (charitable organization receiving the donation)
- An employee of the donor or donee
- Anyone who receives a percentage of the appraised value as compensation

### 3. USPAP Compliance

The **Uniform Standards of Professional Appraisal Practice (USPAP)** are the nationally recognized standards for appraisal practice, established by the Appraisal Foundation and required by Treasury regulations for qualified appraisals.

USPAP requires:

- **Ethics Rule:** Confidentiality, conduct, and management
- **Competency Rule:** Appraiser must have knowledge to perform assignment
- **Scope of Work Rule:** Sufficient research and analysis
- **Reporting Rule:** Clear, accurate, and not misleading appraisal report

**Without USPAP compliance, the IRS will disallow your charitable deduction** even if you have a detailed valuation document.

### 4. Digital Asset Expertise

General real estate or personal property appraisers may not qualify for cryptocurrency valuations unless they demonstrate:

## Treasury Regulation Requirements

### IRC Section 170 Overview

IRC Section 170 allows taxpayers to claim charitable contribution deductions for donations of cash and property to qualified charitable organizations. However, the IRS imposes strict substantiation requirements to prevent overstated valuations.

### Treasury Regulation Section 1.170A-13(c)(3)

This regulation defines "qualified appraisal" and "qualified appraiser" for noncash charitable contributions:

> "A qualified appraisal is an appraisal prepared by a qualified appraiser that meets the requirements of this paragraph (c)(3)... The appraisal must be prepared no earlier than 60 days before the date of the contribution and must be received by the taxpayer on or before the due date (including extensions) for filing the return on which the deduction is claimed."

### Treasury Regulation Section 1.170A-13(c)(4): Appraisal Summary Requirements

The appraisal must include:

1. **Property description:** Sufficient detail to identify the donated assets
2. **Appraised value:** Fair market value determination with supporting analysis
3. **Appraisal date:** Date of valuation (must be within 60 days of donation)
4. **Appraiser credentials:** Qualifications, certification numbers, contact information
5. **Methodology:** Approach used to determine fair market value
6. **Assumptions and limiting conditions:** Any factors affecting the valuation
7. **Signature:** Appraiser's signature and date

### Treasury Regulation Section 1.170A-13(c)(5): Donee Acknowledgment

For Form 8283 Section B, the charitable organization must sign Part IV of Form 8283, acknowledging:

- Receipt of the donated property
- Whether the charity sold, transferred, or still holds the property
- If sold, the date and proceeds from the sale

**Important:** If the charity sells the donated cryptocurrency within three years of receipt, they must file Form 8282, "Donee Information Return," and provide you with a copy.

## Fair Market Value Determination for Digital Assets

### Definition of Fair Market Value

The IRS defines fair market value as:

> "The price at which the property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither having to enter into the transaction, and both having reasonable knowledge of relevant facts."

This definition applies to cryptocurrency and NFTs, but determining FMV for digital assets presents unique challenges compared to traditional assets.

### Cryptocurrency Valuation Methodology

#### 1. Identifying the Valuation Date

The fair market value is determined at the **exact date and time of the donation transaction**. For blockchain transactions, this is the transaction timestamp when the transfer is confirmed on the blockchain.

**Practical Consideration:**
- If you donate Bitcoin at 3:45 PM on December 25, 2025 (UTC), you need the Bitcoin price at that specific timestamp
- Price varies by exchange, trading pair, and liquidity
- QDAV uses on-chain data and multiple exchange sources to determine accurate FMV

#### 2. Exchange Selection Criteria

When selecting which exchange price to use:

- **Primary exchange:** The exchange where the donor typically trades the asset
- **Volume-weighted average:** If using multiple sources, weight by trading volume
- **Liquidity considerations:** For large donations, consider slippage and market impact
- **UTC timestamp:** All valuations use Coordinated Universal Time for consistency

#### 3. Valuation Sources

QDAV uses the following data sources:

- **Major exchanges:** Coinbase, Kraken, Binance, Gemini (for liquid assets like Bitcoin, Ethereum)
- **On-chain data:** Blockchain explorers for transaction verification
- **Market data aggregators:** CoinGecko, CoinMarketCap (as secondary sources)
- **OTC desk prices:** For large institutional transactions
- **Time-weighted average price (TWAP):** For volatile assets to reduce manipulation risk

#### 4. Cryptocurrency Types

**Liquid Assets (Bitcoin, Ethereum):**
- Readily available price data from multiple exchanges
- High daily trading volume
- Minimal slippage for typical donation amounts
- Valuation methodology: Weighted average of exchange prices at transaction timestamp

**Illiquid Tokens:**
- Limited trading volume or no active market
- May have not traded in weeks, months, or years
- Requires discount methodology or alternative valuation approaches
- Valuation methodology: Recent comparable sales, discounted cash flow (for utility tokens), or zero value if no market exists

**Stablecoins (USDC, USDT):**
- Pegged to fiat currency (typically $1 USD)
- Minimal deviation from peg under normal conditions
- Valuation methodology: $1.00 per token, adjusted for any de-pegging events

### NFT Valuation Methodology

NFT valuation presents more complexity than cryptocurrencies due to the unique nature of each token and the importance of traits, rarity, and collection dynamics.

#### 1. Collection-Level Analysis

**Key Metrics:**
- **Floor price:** Lowest listed price in the collection
- **Average sale price (30-day):** Weighted average of recent sales
- **Trading volume:** Total volume over specific periods
- **Holder distribution:** Number of unique holders vs. supply
- **Liquidity:** Number of active listings, time on market

**Sources:** OpenSea, Blur, LooksRare, NFTGo, Dune Analytics

#### 2. Trait-Based Valuation

For high-value NFTs, individual traits significantly affect value:

- **Rarity ranking:** How unique are the traits compared to the collection?
- **Trait scarcity:** What percentage of the collection has these traits?
- **Trait significance:** Are certain traits historically more valuable? (e.g., "Gold Fur" in BAYC)
- **Competitive sales:** Recent sales of NFTs with similar trait combinations

**Methodology:** QDAV uses proprietary algorithms to compare your NFT against recent sales of similar traits, adjusting for market conditions and collection performance.

#### 3. Illiquid NFT Valuation

When an NFT has no recent sales (e.g., held for years, market crash):

- **Floor price adjustment:** Apply discount to current floor price (20-50% for 1+ year without sales)
- **Historical peak adjustment:** Reference highest sale price, discounted for market conditions
- **Comparable sales:** Sales of similar NFTs from same collection (even if not identical traits)
- **Zero value consideration:** If collection is defunct or no market activity, value may be nominal

#### 4. Multiple NFT Considerations

**Group of Similar NFTs:**
- If donating multiple NFTs from the same collection (e.g., 5 Bored Apes), the IRS may treat them as a "group of similar items"
- Individual NFT valuations required if any single NFT exceeds $5,000
- Aggregated value affects Form 8283 section (A vs. B)

**Distinct NFTs:**
- NFTs from different collections are treated as separate items
- Each requires individual valuation if any exceeds $5,000

## Common Pitfalls and IRS Audit Triggers

### Red Flag #1: Overvalued Crypto Donations

**Scenario:** Donor claims Bitcoin donation worth $100,000, but FMV at transaction date was only $60,000.

**IRS Response:** IRS audits high-value charitable deductions, especially for assets with volatile values. If selected for audit:
- You must substantiate the fair market value at donation date
- Without qualified appraisal, deduction disallowed
- Potential penalties for substantial valuation misstatement (20-40% of disallowed amount)

**Mitigation:** QDAV appraisals include detailed FMV analysis with exchange data, transaction verification, and regulatory compliance documentation.

---

### Red Flag #2: Using Price Tracker Screenshots

**Scenario:** Donor prints screenshot from CoinMarketCap showing Bitcoin price of $45,000 and claims this as "appraisal."

**IRS Response:** Price trackers are **not qualified appraisals** because:
- No qualified appraiser performed the valuation
- Not USPAP-compliant
- No appraiser credentials or independence verification
- Cannot withstand audit scrutiny

**Mitigation:** Only accept appraisals from qualified appraisers with USPAP certification and digital asset expertise.

---

### Red Flag #3: Donor-Appraised Self-Valuation

**Scenario:** Donor researches Bitcoin price, determines $45,000 FMV, and completes their own "appraisal form."

**IRS Response:** Self-appraisals are explicitly prohibited under Treasury Regulation Section 1.170A-13(c)(3). The regulation requires an independent qualified appraiser with no relationship to the donor.

**Mitigation:** Hire independent QDAV for unbiased, compliant valuation.

---

### Red Flag #4: Incorrect Valuation Date

**Scenario:** Donor donated Bitcoin on December 20, 2025, but uses price from December 31, 2025 (year-end).

**IRS Response:** FMV must be determined at the **exact date of donation**, not a convenient date. December 20 vs. December 31 could represent a 20%+ price difference in crypto markets.

**Mitigation:** QDAV uses blockchain transaction timestamps and exchange data at the precise time of donation.

---

### Red Flag #5: Aggregation Errors

**Scenario:** Donor has 3 wallets with Bitcoin totaling $60,000, files Form 8283 Section A (under $5,000 each), claiming no appraisal needed.

**IRS Response:** If the Bitcoin wallets are "similar items" (same asset type, held for same purpose), they may be aggregated. If combined value exceeds $5,000, Section B and qualified appraisal required.

**Mitigation:** QDAV provides guidance on aggregation rules and proper Form 8283 completion.

---

### Red Flag #6: Missing Documentation

**Scenario:** Donor obtains qualified appraisal but fails to get charity's signature on Form 8283 Part IV, or loses the appraisal before filing.

**IRS Response:** Incomplete Form 8283 or missing appraisal = deduction disallowed. No exceptions.

**Mitigation:** QDAV coordinates with the charitable organization to ensure all signatures and acknowledgments are obtained before filing.

## Self-Directed IRA Crypto Appraisals: Separate Requirements

### Understanding Self-Directed IRAs

A Self-Directed IRA (SDIRA) is an Individual Retirement Account that allows investment in alternative assets, including cryptocurrency. Unlike traditional IRAs held by major custodians (Fidelity, Vanguard), SDIRAs permit direct ownership of digital assets through specialized custodians like Bitcoin IRA, Equity Trust, or Domus Trust.

### Annual Appraisal Requirements

**Why Appraise SDIRA Crypto?**

1. **IRS Form 5498 Reporting:** IRA custodians must report the fair market value of all IRA assets to the IRS annually (due May 31)
2. **Prohibited Transaction Rules:** IRS must verify no disallowed self-dealing or transactions with disqualified persons
3. **Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs):** Starting at age 73, SDIRA owners must take RMDs; FMV determines distribution value

**When Appraisal Required:**
- **Annual valuation:** Every December 31 (year-end FMV for Form 5498)
- **In-kind distributions:** When taking RMDs in cryptocurrency form
- **Roth conversions:** Converting traditional SDIRA to Roth (taxable event based on FMV)

### SDIRA Appraisal vs. Charitable Donation Appraisal

| Factor | SDIRA Appraisal | Charitable Donation Appraisal |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------------------|
| **Purpose** | Annual reporting to IRS Form 5498 | Charitable contribution deduction |
| **Regulation** | IRA custodian requirements | IRC Section 170, Treasury Reg 1.170A-13 |
| **Form** | Form 5498 (filed by custodian) | Form 8283 (filed by donor) |
| **Deadline** | December 31 valuation | Within 60 days of donation |
| **Appraiser Requirements** | Custodian may specify | Must be qualified appraiser per Treasury Reg |
| **Form 8283** | Not required | Required if over $5,000 |

**Important:** A charitable donation qualified appraisal **does not satisfy** SDIRA annual reporting requirements. SDIRA appraisals may have different standards based on the custodian's policies. Consult your SDIRA custodian for their specific requirements.

## NFT Valuation: Special Considerations

### NFT Market Dynamics

The NFT market differs fundamentally from cryptocurrency markets:

- **Unique assets:** Each NFT is distinct; no two are identical (even from same collection)
- **Trait-based value:** Specific attributes (background, fur, eyes, accessories) drive value
- **Collection volatility:** Collections can boom or bust based on trends, celebrity endorsements, or platform changes
- **Liquidity risk:** Many NFTs may not sell for months or years
- **Marketplace fragmentation:** Sales occur on OpenSea, Blur, LooksRare, Foundation, and other platforms

### Valuation Challenges

#### 1. No Active Market

**Scenario:** Donor acquired a Bored Ape in January 2022 at peak (mint price + premium), now holds through market downturn.

**Issues:**
- No recent sales of this specific NFT
- Collection floor price dropped 70%+ from peak
- What is "fair" market value in depressed market?

**QDAV Approach:**
- Analyze 30-90 day comparable sales (similar traits)
- Apply discount for lack of marketability if no recent activity
- Document market conditions and collection performance
- Consider historical sales data with time-weighted adjustments

#### 2. Trait Anomalies

**Scenario:** Donor has BAYC #8817 with rare "Golden Fur" trait, worth significantly more than floor price.

**Issues:**
- Floor price irrelevant for rare trait combinations
- Need to identify comparable sales of rare traits
- May need to value based on highest comparable sale, not average

**QDAV Approach:**
- Rarity ranking analysis from Rarity Sniper, Trait Sniper, or similar tools
- Recent sales of NFTs with similar trait profiles
- Expert judgment on trait significance in collection context

#### 3. New Collections (Post-Mint)

**Scenario:** Donor received NFT from recent mint (weeks or months old), no established secondary market.

**Issues:**
- No sales history for comparable analysis
- Mint price may not reflect current FMV
- High risk of collection failure or value collapse

**QDAV Approach:**
- Compare mint price to current floor (if secondary market exists)
- Analyze team credibility, community strength, utility promises
- Apply risk discount if collection showing signs of decline
- Consider zero value if collection abandoned

#### 4. Fractionalized NFTs

**Scenario:** Donor owns 10% ownership share of an NFT split across multiple token holders.

**Issues:**
- Fractionalized ownership complicates valuation
- Liquidity may be extremely limited
- Platform risk (fractionalization platform could fail)

**QDAV Approach:**
- Value underlying NFT (entire token)
- Apply percentage ownership
- Discount for lack of marketability and platform risk

#### Q: What if the cryptocurrency was acquired over multiple dates at different prices?

**A:** For charitable donations, the **acquisition cost basis is irrelevant**. The IRS only cares about fair market value at the date of donation (for capital gain property, you avoid paying capital gains tax on the appreciation).

- You bought 1 Bitcoin for $10,000 in 2020
- You bought 1 Bitcoin for $30,000 in 2024
- You donate both on December 15, 2025, when BTC = $45,000

**FMV for deduction:** 2 BTC × $45,000 = $90,000 (not your $40,000 cost basis)

QDAV appraises the FMV at donation date, not your acquisition cost.
