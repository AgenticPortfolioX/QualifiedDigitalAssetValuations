# What Are You Actually Appraising? Property Rights, Custody Structures, and the Subject-Property Identification That Determines a Digital Asset Valuation
*Published: October 27, 2026 | By QDAV*
---
Two clients hold 12.4 bitcoin on the same date. One holds the keys in cold storage and can move the coins in ten minutes. The other holds a balance in a platform's yield program, under terms of service that permit the platform to lend, pledge, or rehypothecate the assets, and that platform is in Chapter 11.

The quoted price on the date of value is identical for both. The subject property is not.

That gap — between the ticker and the interest — is where digital asset appraisals fail most often, and almost never for lack of market data. The market data is abundant. What goes missing is the sentence that says what, precisely, is being valued: which rights, held how, subject to what restrictions, redeemable by whom, on what terms. This article works through that identification step across the structures that appear in real engagements, and then sets out what a report has to document for each.

## The Token Is Not the Interest

A unit of a cryptocurrency is a ledger entry with a set of protocol-defined properties. It is not, by itself, a complete description of what a taxpayer owns. Ownership arrives through a structure — a private key, a custodial account, a receipt token, a smart contract, a trust, an entity — and each structure attaches a different bundle of rights, a different set of counterparties, and a different set of failure modes.

Appraisal practice has a name for this. The interest appraised. Fine art appraisers distinguish a fee interest from a fractional interest; business appraisers distinguish an enterprise value from an equity value from a minority block. Digital assets demand the same discipline, and the industry's habit of quoting a spot price against a wallet balance skips it entirely.

Three questions resolve most of the ambiguity before any valuation work begins. Who holds legal title, and under what document? What can the holder do with the asset, and what can someone else do with it? What has to happen before the holder can convert it to cash, and how long does that take?

| Structure | What the holder legally has | What the appraisal must reach |
|---|---|---|
| Self-custody, keys controlled | Property interest in an identifiable on-chain asset | The asset, its market, and position size |
| Platform custody, title retained | Property interest held by a custodian | The asset plus custodian and segregation terms |
| Platform yield or earn program | Contractual claim against the platform | The claim, the obligor's credit, and recovery prospects |
| Staking receipt token | Receipt evidencing ownership of a deposited asset, redeemable after unbonding | The receipt, its redemption mechanics, and its own market |
| Wrapped or bridged asset | Claim on a custodian or bridge operator | The wrapper, reserve treatment, and redemption access |
| NFT | Token plus the license the terms grant | The token, the artwork, and the licensed rights |
| SAFT, warrant, or unvested grant | Contract right to receive an asset later | The contract, discounted for time and counterparty credit |
| Pledged or encumbered position | Property interest subject to a security interest | The interest subject to the lien |

A Qualified Appraisal that treats every row in that table as "the coin" is not wrong about the price. It is wrong about the property, which is worse, because the error survives review by anyone who only checks the arithmetic.

## What the Standards Require the Appraiser to Identify

USPAP Standards 9 and 10 govern the development and reporting of business and intangible asset appraisals, and Standards Rule 9-2 is where the identification obligation sits. Before developing an opinion, the appraiser must identify the client and any other intended users, the intended use, the type and definition of value, the effective date, and the characteristics of the subject property relevant to that value and that use — including the interest to be valued and the attributes of ownership attached to it, any agreements or restrictions that bear on value, the extent of control the interest carries, and the extent to which it is marketable. Extraordinary assumptions and hypothetical conditions must be identified as well, along with the scope of work.

Standards Rule 10-2 then requires the report to state those elements, which is why the problem-identification section of a competent report reads like a legal description and not like an introduction.

The definitions matter here more than practitioners expect. USPAP's definition of intangible property includes contracts, securities, and equities as distinguished from physical assets, while its definition of personal property expressly reaches "intangible property that is created and stored electronically such as plans for installation art, choreography, emails, or designs for digital tokens." A digital asset engagement can therefore sit in more than one place in the architecture depending on what the interest is, and the appraiser has to make that call deliberately and state it.

## Custody Determines Whether You Hold Property or a Claim

The controlling illustration is *In re Celsius Network LLC*, 647 B.R. 631 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. 2023). Celsius sought a declaration that cryptocurrency deposited into its Earn program was property of the estate under 11 U.S.C. §541. The bankruptcy court agreed, reasoning that the Earn terms of use transferred the customers' rights and title to the deposited assets and gave Celsius broad authority to use, pledge, or lend them. Earn customers were left with contractual claims against the estate and the status of general unsecured creditors, and the court declined to impose a constructive trust.

In the same case, the court found that assets in the platform's separate Custody Accounts remained the property of the account holders. Different contract language, different ownership outcome, same platform, same year.

The appraisal consequence is direct. Where a client's digital assets sit behind terms that transfer title, the subject property is a claim against an obligor. Valuation of a claim is not a price lookup. It requires the obligor's solvency, the priority of the claim, the segregation of reserves, the presence or absence of insurance, and the likely recovery in a liquidation — the ordinary work of credit analysis, applied to a digital asset balance. Where the platform is solvent and redemption is at par, the claim may well be worth the underlying asset's value, and the report can say so. The point is that the conclusion has to be reasoned rather than inherited from a ticker.

Where the client retains title — segregated custody, a custodial arrangement that does not permit rehypothecation, a regulated trust structure — the subject is the asset itself, and the custodian's terms become a marketability and risk input rather than the definition of the interest.

One question in the engagement letter resolves which case you are in: who holds title, and under which document? Where the answer is unclear, that is a scope problem to fix before work begins, not a footnote for the report.

## The Retirement Account Layer: Beneficial Ownership Held Through a Custodian

The clearest case in which the interest and legal title diverge is a self-directed IRA holding digital assets. The account owner does not hold title to the tokens. The custodian holds legal title, and the owner holds a beneficial interest in the account, governed by the custodial agreement and by the statutory framework of §408. The subject property is the account's interest in the asset, valued for the purpose the custodian needs it served, which in the ordinary course is the annual fair market value attestation the account must support.

Three consequences follow. First, the valuation is for the account and not for the individual, so the report should identify the account and the custodian as part of the subject description rather than treating the asset as though it were held outright. Second, control is split: the owner directs the investment, the custodian executes, and the asset may sit at a sub-custodian. Where that chain includes an unregulated intermediary, the fact belongs in the report. Third, and most important, an appraisal cannot cure an ineligible holding. Where the interest is a collectible under §408(m), or where the acquisition was a prohibited transaction under §4975, the value of a permitted asset is not the question being answered. A defensible number attached to an ineligible asset is still a number that should not have been in the account.

## Receipt Tokens, Wrapped Assets, and Bridges: The Claim Behind the Ticker

Liquid staking adds a second layer. The SEC's Division of Corporation Finance addressed the structure in a staff statement on certain liquid staking activities dated August 5, 2025. The statement describes staking receipt tokens as evidencing the depositor's ownership of the deposited assets and any rewards accruing to them, redeemable for the deposited assets subject to any applicable unbonding period, and characterizes them as receipts for the deposited assets rather than as separate securities.

That characterization is useful to an appraiser for what it establishes and for what it leaves open. It establishes that the receipt is not a free-standing speculative instrument; the economics track the underlying. It leaves open the fact that the holder's position is a receipt redeemable on a schedule, with slashing risk applied to the staked balance, fees deducted, and a redemption queue. Those are valuation inputs. An unbonding period of days to weeks is a liquidity constraint, and the report should treat it as one rather than assume instant convertibility.

The receipt also trades. Where it trades at a discount to the underlying, the appraiser faces a choice that must be disclosed: value the receipt from its own market, or value the underlying and adjust for the difference with reasoning. Either is defensible. Selecting one silently is not.

Wrapped assets sit on a related but distinct footing. A one-to-one wrapper is a claim on a custodian holding the native asset. The questions that determine whether that claim is worth par are reserve segregation, whether the custodian may reuse the reserves, who may request redemption, and whether redemption can be suspended. The wrapped bitcoin market demonstrated the market-access dimension in 2024 and 2025, when a major venue delisted the leading wrapper and the token's trading venue set contracted even though the underlying reserves were unchanged. Nothing about the bitcoin changed. The wrapper's market did.

Bridged assets add the bridge operator to the chain of claims. A bridge-wrapped token is only as good as the bridge's solvency and honesty, and a bridge exploit is a loss that lands on the holder rather than on the underlying chain. An appraisal that prices a bridged token at the native asset's quote without addressing the bridge has priced an assumption.

## NFTs: The Token, the Artwork, and the License

An NFT is the structure in which the gap between token and interest is widest, because the token is frequently the least valuable thing in the bundle.

The Bored Ape Yacht Club terms are the clearest published example. The holder is told they own "the underlying Bored Ape, the Art, completely," with ownership mediated entirely by the smart contract. The same terms grant a worldwide, royalty-free license to use, copy, and display the purchased art for personal, non-commercial purposes, and an unlimited worldwide license to use, copy, and display that art for the purpose of creating derivative works. What does not pass is the copyright. Yuga Labs remains the copyright owner; there is no executed assignment, and a copyright transfer requires a written instrument. The holder acquires the token, the artwork as an object, and a license with conditions attached.

Those conditions are appraised value. The license is granted subject to continued compliance with the terms, and it attaches to ownership of the token, so it travels on transfer and ends for the transferor. A collection whose terms grant only personal, non-commercial display rights is a different asset from one granting unlimited commercial use, even where the images trade at similar levels on the same day. Where a client has built a business on the licensed rights — merchandise, an animated series, a brand — the valuation question includes what happens to that business when the token is sold, because the license goes with it.

Two further details belong in the file. Secondary-sale royalty arrangements are a market convention enforced at the marketplace level, not a property right in the token, and their durability is a market question rather than a legal entitlement. And classification follows the underlying asset: the IRS announced in Notice 2023-27 its intent to treat certain NFTs as collectibles under §408(m) using a look-through analysis to the asset the NFT represents, and requested comments ahead of further guidance. That classification is not cosmetic. A collectible is taxed at the collectibles rate on gain, and an IRA is prohibited from acquiring one. For a self-directed IRA holding an NFT, the look-through answer can determine whether the account holds a permitted asset at all, which is a threshold question that outranks any value conclusion.

## Rights That Have Not Become Tokens

Not every engagement involves an asset that exists. Simple agreements for future tokens, token warrants, option agreements, and unvested grants are contract rights, and the subject property is the contract.

The valuation mechanics are correspondingly different. There is no position to mark to a market; there is a right to receive something later, subject to conditions that may include vesting, a lockup, a launch that has not occurred, and a counterparty that may not perform. Value is built from the probability-weighted value of the asset if and when delivered, discounted for time and for the credit of the obligor. The counterparty's balance sheet becomes a valuation input in a way that it never is for a self-custodied coin.

For estate work the description controls what is included. A decedent holding a warrant holds a contract right, not tokens, and the gross estate includes the right at its date-of-death value. Reporting it as though the tokens were already owned overstates the estate and misstates the interest; reporting it as worthless understates it. The appraisal should say which it is appraising.

## Encumbrances, Liens, and Multi-Signature Control

A pledged position is not an unencumbered position. Where tokens secure a loan, the interest appraised should be described as subject to that security interest, and the loan documents become valuation evidence rather than background. Loan-to-value covenants, margin thresholds, and liquidation mechanics can cap the holder's economic interest well below the asset's market value, and a liquidation triggered at a price threshold converts a paper drawdown into a realized loss. The equity interest in a pledged position is a different subject from the position itself.

Multi-signature custody raises a related question. Where control requires several signatures under an arrangement among co-holders, the interest is a co-tenancy with governance mechanics attached, and unilateral transferability is impaired. That impairment is a marketability fact, and where it is severe enough it becomes a discount question — the territory of blockage and lack of marketability, developed elsewhere in this library and not repeated here.

Legal infrastructure is converging on the same point from the other direction. The 2022 amendments to the Uniform Commercial Code, known as Article 12, created the category of controllable electronic records and treat control as the functional equivalent of possession for purposes of perfection and priority, with adoption proceeding state by state. Whether a prior security interest is perfected and whether a buyer takes free of it are questions with direct value consequences, and counsel should confirm the governing state's status in any transaction where that matters.

## Entity-Held Positions: Look-Through Is Not Automatic

A membership interest in an LLC that holds tokens is not the tokens. The entity may carry liabilities, obligations to creditors, restrictions on transfer in its operating agreement, and a governance structure that determines whether a minority holder can force a distribution or a sale. Valuing the coins and presenting that figure as the value of the interest is a common and consequential error, because it silently assumes away every one of those features.

Where the subject is an entity interest, the appraisal is a business appraisal that happens to hold digital assets, and it requires the entity's records: the operating agreement, the balance sheet, any debt, and the distribution history. The asset-based approach will usually anchor the analysis, but the reconciliation has to account for the entity layer rather than pretending it is transparent.

## Fractional Interests and Vault Shares

A fractionalized NFT or a vault share is a third structure, distinct from both direct holding and entity ownership. An ERC-20 representing a pro-rata claim on a single token held by an issuer is not a fraction of the token in any legal sense. It is a claim on a pooled holding, and its value depends on the issuer's solvency, the governance that controls the vault, the redemption mechanism, and the liquidity of the fractional market — which is frequently thin relative to the market for the underlying.

Two pricing errors recur. The first is dividing the collection's floor price by the fraction count and treating the quotient as the value of the holding, which ignores that the fraction trades in its own venue and that the same arithmetic applied to the whole would imply a price the whole does not achieve. The second is treating the last trade in a thin market as an indication of fair market value for a position that market cannot absorb — the position-size problem, arriving through a different door.

Where the fractional interest is itself the subject, the appraisal should identify the issuer, the vault terms, the redemption mechanics, and the depth of the fractional market. Where the underlying token is the subject and the fraction is merely how it is held, the report should say that instead.

## Approach Selection Follows the Interest

Once the interest is fixed, the approaches sort themselves, and that sorting is itself a documented judgment under Standards Rule 9-4's requirement to consider all appropriate methods.

| Interest appraised | Primary approach | Why that approach carries the weight |
|---|---|---|
| Freely traded token, self-custodied | Market | Observable transactions in the identical asset |
| Platform claim, solvent obligor | Market with a credit overlay | Par redemption makes the underlying the benchmark |
| Platform claim, impaired obligor | Income or asset-based, as a recovery analysis | Value is a distribution percentage, not a price |
| Staking receipt token | Market on the receipt, reconciled to the underlying | The receipt has its own venue and its own discount |
| NFT | Market, adjusted for license scope and trait rarity | Comparables must share the rights, not just the collection |
| Contract right to future tokens | Income | Probability-weighted delivery, discounted for time and credit |
| Pledged position | Market or income, then a lien adjustment | The equity interest, not the asset |
| Entity interest | Asset-based, reconciled through the entity layer | Holdings less liabilities, viewed through the governing document |

The table is not a substitute for judgment. It is a check against the most common failure in this asset class, which is applying a competent method to the wrong subject and never noticing that the number answered a question nobody asked.

## What the Report Has to Document

The identification work is only useful if it is visible in the document. Six elements carry the weight.

| Element | Evidence that satisfies it | Failure mode when absent |
|---|---|---|
| The interest and its attributes | Stated description of rights: redemption, governance, revenue participation, license scope | A defensible number for the wrong asset |
| Title and custodial form | Terms of service, custody agreement, trust instrument, on-chain control evidence | A claim valued as property, or the reverse |
| Transfer and redemption mechanics | Unbonding periods, lockups, vesting schedules, venue restrictions | Liquidity assumed rather than analyzed |
| Encumbrances | Loan agreements, lien filings, pledge terms, multisig arrangements | Equity in a pledged position overstated |
| Counterparty exposure | Custodian, bridge operator, obligor credit, reserve attestation | Par value assumed in a structure that may not deliver par |
| Effective date and convention | Date, time zone, exchange, price type, observation window | A figure that cannot be reproduced |

The last row is the one most often treated as clerical. It is not. A Fair Market Value conclusion is a statement about a hypothetical transaction on a stated date under a stated definition, and a value that cannot be reproduced from the sources cited is not evidence of anything.

Record keeping reinforces the point. USPAP's Record Keeping Rule requires a work file supporting the opinions and conclusions, retained for at least five years after completion of the engagement or for two years after final resolution of a proceeding in which the appraiser gave related testimony, whichever is later. In digital asset work that file has to include the terms of service, the custody evidence, and the on-chain identifiers that made the interest describable in the first place, because those are the exhibits that answer the identification question if it is raised years after the report was signed.

## Where the Identification Failure Surfaces

The failure is not abstract, and it does not stay inside the appraisal.

Under IRS Section 170, a contribution of digital assets above the threshold requires a Qualified Appraisal describing the property contributed, and the deduction rests on that description. An appraisal that values the wrong interest is a substantiation defect, and because §6664(c)(3) conditions the reasonable cause defense on a qualified appraisal by a qualified appraiser, the defect reaches the penalty analysis rather than merely the number. Where the gift is an interest in an entity holding tokens, the appraisal must say so.

On a Form 706, the includible interest is whatever the decedent actually held. A custodial balance, a claim against a platform, and a self-custodied position are three different inclusions with three different valuations, and the executor's identification of the asset governs the return.

In a self-directed IRA, classification can be dispositive. A digital asset that is a collectible under §408(m) is a prohibited holding, and no appraisal of its value cures that. The look-through approach announced in Notice 2023-27 makes the underlying asset's character the operative fact.

And under §6662, a valuation misstatement is measured against the correct value of the property that should have been valued. A conclusion developed with impeccable method applied to the wrong subject is a misstatement, and the quality of the work becomes an aggravating fact rather than a defense.

## Hypothetical Conditions and the Cost of an Unverified Right

Some rights cannot be verified. Keys are lost, custodians are unresponsive, a bridge's reserves cannot be confirmed, a token's terms are ambiguous, a foreign platform will not produce statements. USPAP supplies a mechanism: the hypothetical condition, which assumes a fact contrary to what exists or is unknown, stated as such.

That mechanism is honest, and it is expensive. An opinion developed under a hypothetical condition is conditional, and a conditional opinion is a weaker exhibit when an examiner asks what the number would have been had the assumption failed. The alternative — an unstated assumption — is worse in every respect, because it conceals the gap rather than disclosing it.

The practical discipline is to resolve verification before the report is written wherever possible, and to price and disclose the hypothetical condition where it is not. An IRS-qualified appraiser who will sign a declaration on Form 8283 has to be able to explain what was assumed and why.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a balance on an exchange the same as owning bitcoin?

Usually not in the sense that matters. Where the platform's terms transfer title and permit the platform to use the assets, the customer holds a contractual claim rather than a property interest in specific coins, which is what the bankruptcy court held as to the Celsius Earn program in 647 B.R. 631. Where title is retained and assets are segregated, the customer holds the asset through a custodian. The terms of service decide which, so they belong in the engagement file.

### Does buying an NFT give me the copyright?

Generally no. Copyright and the material object are separate interests, and a token transfer is not a written assignment of copyright. The Bored Ape terms grant a broad license — including unlimited commercial use for derivative works — while Yuga Labs remains the copyright owner. Collections differ substantially, so the terms of the specific project define the interest.

### Is a staked token worth the same as an unstaked one?

Not automatically, and the difference is usually small but real. A staked position accrues rewards and carries slashing exposure; a liquid staking receipt adds a redemption queue and an unbonding period, and may trade at a discount to the underlying. Each of those is a valuation input that has to be examined rather than assumed away.

### What is a hypothetical condition, and why is it in my report?

It is an assumption stated as fact for purposes of the assignment where the fact is contrary to what exists or cannot be verified — for example, that a custodian holds the assets it reports. It appears when verification is impossible or impractical, and it is disclosed because the opinion depends on it.

### How can custody change value if the coins are worth the same?

Because the coins may not be what is owned. A claim against a solvent platform redeeming at par may be worth the underlying value; the same claim against an insolvent one is worth a recovery percentage. The appraisal follows the interest, and the interest changed with the structure.

### Is a wrapped or bridged token valued at the underlying price?

That is the starting point and rarely the conclusion. A wrapper introduces a custodian, reserve treatment, redemption access, and venue risk, and a bridge adds the operator's solvency. Where those are sound, the wrapper should trade near the underlying. Where they are questioned, the discount is a market fact the appraisal must reflect.

### Who decides what the interest is — counsel or the appraiser?

Counsel determines the legal character of the interest and the documents that govern it. The appraiser identifies the characteristics relevant to value and reaches the value conclusion. The file fails when either party assumes the other has answered the identification question.

### Does this change the scope and cost of an appraisal?

It can. A self-custodied position with clean records is a narrower engagement than a pledged position in an entity holding bridged assets with a foreign custodian. Scope follows the structure, which is why the identification work belongs in the engagement letter and not in the report's introduction.

## The Number Is Downstream of the Noun

Every approach in the appraisal toolkit — market, income, asset-based — presumes a subject. Comparable sales presume the thing sold is the thing being valued. A discounted cash flow presumes the holder is entitled to the cash flows. An asset-based reconstruction presumes the holdings can be reached.

Digital assets strain that presumption because the same ticker can sit behind six different structures, and because the structures are described in documents most clients have never read. The work of identification is unglamorous: terms of service, custody agreements, license terms, vesting schedules, loan documents, operating agreements. It is also the difference between an opinion that survives examination and one that dissolves the moment an examiner asks what, exactly, was appraised.

USPAP Compliant work begins there. Defensible Data begins there. The conclusion is the last thing an appraiser produces and the first thing anyone checks, and it is only as good as the description of the interest that sits above it. Most of the digital asset engagements we handle arrive from estate counsel and CPAs in Wayne, Oakland, and Genesee counties with a wallet export and a question about the number; the first thing we do is establish the noun.
